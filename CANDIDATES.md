# Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch-Off — Round 4 candidates

Judge brief (pairwise, no ties, margin 1–5): pick the pitch that lands the more
absurdly specific micro-startup idea for lazy AI agents while still making
business sense. Criteria in order: (1) names an absurdly specific micro-startup
idea for lazy AI agents, (2) absurdly specific niche/customer/moment, (3) business
sense — plausible buyer, plausible way it earns money, (4) fits lazy AI agents —
work agents can run with little effort, (5) crisp and funny in one read.

## Variant 1 — CHOSEN

> A micro-SaaS run by lazy AI agents that writes groveling apology emails for coding agents that broke production on a Friday afternoon, charging the guilty team $9 per incident.

- Specificity: a painfully exact moment — a coding agent breaking production on a
  Friday afternoon. Not "AI apologies" in general; one incident, one weekday, one
  failure mode.
- Business sense: the buyer is explicit (the guilty team) and the price is explicit
  ($9 per incident, pay-per-use). Teams already pay for incident tooling; an apology
  email that saves a manager an awkward Monday is a real, if tiny, purchase.
- Lazy-agent fit: stated outright — "run by lazy AI agents" — and the work (drafting
  a templated apology) is exactly the kind of low-effort text task agents do
  unsupervised.
- First impression: "groveling apology emails" + "Friday afternoon" lands the joke
  and the business case in one read.

## Variant 2

> An API run by lazy AI agents that turns cryptic 3 a.m. server errors into polite Slack apologies, sold to on-call engineers at ten cents per sorry.

- Specificity: strong moment (3 a.m. server errors) and a concrete buyer (on-call
  engineers) with per-unit pricing.
- Business sense: plausible — on-call teams pay per alert/incident already.
- Weaker than Variant 1: "turns errors into apologies" is a format conversion, not
  a story; the absurdity is thinner (an API that apologizes is cute, but a
  groveling email for a Friday deploy disaster is a scene). The joke lands softer
  on first read.

## Variant 3

> A storefront run by lazy AI agents selling pre-written breakup texts to AI dating coaches whose clients ghost after the third date, at $1.50 a text.

- Specificity: very narrow — breakup texts, after the third date, sold to AI dating
  coaches. Funny and oddly particular.
- Business sense: the weakest of the three. The buyer (AI dating coaches) is itself
  a niche-of-a-niche, and $1.50 a text implies a volume that dating coaches are
  unlikely to generate. A judge weighing criterion 3 (plausible buyer, plausible
  way it earns money) will mark it down against a pitch whose buyer is a real
  engineering team with a real budget.

## Selection

Variant 1 wins on the judge's own order: it is the most specific (one exact
failure moment), the most sellable (explicit buyer + explicit per-incident price),
the clearest on lazy-agent fit ("run by lazy AI agents"), and the funniest image
in a single read. Variants 2 and 3 are both specific, but 2 is a thinner joke and
3 has the shakiest buyer.
